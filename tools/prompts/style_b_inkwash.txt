Four characters from the 17th-century Korean classical novel "Sassi Namjeonggi", whose story is set in Ming-dynasty China (16th century, Jiajing era). Therefore everyone wears MING DYNASTY Chinese Han clothing, NOT Korean hanbok. They stand side by side, full body, facing the viewer, evenly spaced, same scale, on a plain background. From left to right:
1) Lady Sa, the virtuous principal wife, early 20s, calm and dignified, gentle clear eyes, hair in a neat high bun with a few modest jade pins, a long muted indigo-blue and pale celadon Ming-style beizi coat over a long pleated skirt, hands folded politely in front.
2) Yu Yeon-su, her husband, a young Hanlin academician in his early 20s, handsome but with a slightly indecisive, easily swayed expression, black Ming official gauze hat (wusha cap with two side wings), dark green round-collar official robe with a square embroidered rank badge on the chest and a belt.
3) Lady Gyo, the young concubine, about 17, very beautiful with a subtly calculating sidelong smile, elaborate hairstyle with many gold hairpins and a red flower, bright peach-pink and crimson layered Ming dress with gold trim, holding a round silk fan.
4) Dong Cheong, the scheming household secretary, late 20s, thin face, narrow cunning eyes, thin moustache, square black scholar cap, plain grey-brown scholar robe, holding a calligraphy brush.
Each character must be instantly distinguishable by silhouette and main color. Absolutely no text, no letters, no labels, no seals, no signatures, no captions.

Art style: Korean ink-wash painting with light watercolor tints (sumuk damchae): expressive calligraphic brush lines, soft transparent color washes, generous white space, visible hanji paper texture, elegant and restrained, like a refined hand-painted illustrated book.
